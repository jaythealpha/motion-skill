## Follow-up: connected roofs and consistent seating — 2026-11-04

The earlier equal-radius check did not establish that the mirrored halves met. Separate great-circle ridge interpolation created a large unintended gap (up to21.12m on the largest model patch). Replaced it with one shared small-circle ridge in the centre plane and a shared constant-level springing edge, followed by spherical interpolation across each half. Both the75m sphere and70m ridge working radii are explicit modelling assumptions. Regression tests now check radius, finite/envelope bounds, matching mirrored ridges and level springing edges. This repairs mathematical connectivity; it does not certify fidelity to surveyed roof cuts. Shared ridge ribs/caps are generated once; free panel edges have thickness and the joined centre is not duplicated as an internal wall.

The estimated fan seating now shares one coordinate function with curved floor treads; chairs face the south stage. Existing straight treads did not support every curved seat. Added chair backs, arms/supports and solid balcony fronts; left the centre of the timber ceiling section open so it does not obscure the hall. Its profile is still an estimated cutaway, not a measured ceiling plan.

Consulted ARM's direct project account (https://armarchitecture.com.au/projects/sydney-opera-house-concert-hall-renewal/): horseshoe automated stage risers, acoustic-diffusing box fronts, an eastern-side passage and a new lift in the Northern Foyer. Corrected the previous west-side access diagram; exact lift dimensions, coordinates and route remain estimates and are not visitor navigation. The illustrative horseshoe lift configuration and diffuser treatment are now shown only for the renewal state, not1973. Source descriptions do not establish that this configuration is deployed at every performance; no operational motion or safety guidance is provided. Retained people's records, scoped counts and historical endpoint.
