# Seoul civil-history examples — evidence and geometry boundaries

Researched 2026-11-03. Generator: `seoul-civil-gen.mjs`. This is educational structural history, not a validated structural solver, surveyed CAD, emergency instruction or a certificate of safety. No people are drawn. The Seongsu update includes generic normal-traffic vehicles before collapse and after reopening, not accident vehicle identities or fall trajectories. No downloaded photographs, scanned plans, third-party meshes or source prose are included. All five narrative language packs are authored in the generator.

## Sampoong (29 June–19 July 1995)

- [National Archives subject account](https://www.archives.go.kr/next/newsearch/listSubjectDescription.do?id=001042&sitePage=) describes the morning fifth-floor cracks, flat-slab/column failure, added roof loading and A-wing collapse. Its authored subject explanation is not itself an original as-built plan or forensic simulation.
- [National Archives historical overview](https://theme.archives.go.kr/next/koreaOfRecord/bigAccident.do) identifies the collapsed A wing and final death toll 502. No victims are rendered.
- Approximate time differs: the Archives account uses 17:55; [Seoul's retrospective](https://mediahub.seoul.go.kr/archives/2002100) uses 17:57. Keep the difference visible. The 17:55–17:57 chapter is a display interval, **not** a measured two-minute collapse duration.
- [National History record](https://db.history.go.kr/id/tcct_1995_07_15_0030) dates the final live rescue to 15 July; [Presidential Archives](https://www.pa.go.kr/research/contents/policy/index15.jsp?poMode=view&policySeq=3045) records the 19 July disaster-area declaration. July 15/19 event entries are date anchors, not assertions of midnight execution.
- The 08:00 opening is a presentation bound, not a recorded event; its card says scene context rather than a recorded timestamp. No day-by-day debris-clearance schedule or completed clearing is asserted. No rebuilt department store is invented. The residual-site scene is held after collapse.
- Geometry is explicitly estimated: a nominal 133×70m scene envelope (including adjacent context wing),26m height envelope, five aboveground study levels, four illustrative subgrade tiers, positions, column grid, slab subdivisions, roof plant, facade pattern, cracks and fragment paths. The context wing remains as context; its depiction does not certify safety. The reference material does not establish the model's grid or basement measurements.
- Explore hides exterior panels/selected roof cells for column/slab inspection. This is an explanatory cutaway, not a dated missing facade or a real access route.

## Seongsu (21 October 1994–3 July 1997)

- [National Archives](https://theme.archives.go.kr/next/koreaOfRecord/bigAccident.do): original length 1160.8m and width 19.4m, four lanes, Gerber truss, central 48m loss. The scene uses road **above** the trusses, not a through-truss bridge.
- [CODIL facility-safety report](https://www.codil.or.kr/filebank/original/RK/OTKCRK190878/OTKCRK190878.pdf):48m suspended segment within the 120m span between piers 10 and 11. [Seoul's 20-year review](https://news.seoul.go.kr/safe/archives/28907?listPage=1) also describes the 48m loss and approximately 07:40, with 32 fatalities and 17 injuries. Source times are approximate; another [CODIL account](https://www.codil.or.kr/filebank/original/RK/OTMCRK500958/OTMCRK500958.pdf) uses 07:38. The UI adopts Seoul's approximate 07:40, never exact seconds. The displayed 07:40–07:41 interval is not a measured fall duration.
- [Contemporaneous reopening report](https://www.yna.co.kr/view/AKR19970703001400004) dates works to 26 April 1995 and reopening/traffic to 3 July 1997 noon, and describes replacement of the **whole deck and truss**, not a 48m patch. Facts are independently paraphrased, no article text/images copied. [Seoul's 2004 expansion announcement](https://mediahub.seoul.go.kr/archives/145526) independently confirms the 1997-07-03 four-lane reopening and distinguishes subsequent widening.
- The final state is 1997's four lanes. No modern eight-lane bridge or later ramps are retroactively shown. Intermediate erection stages are illustrative, not dated construction evidence. Piers/water/support profiles are schematic; retention on screen does not assert their exact historical repair treatment.
- The 07:30 opening and 07:41 aftermath bound are labelled scene context, not recorded event timestamps. Only the 48m central upper section drops. Adjacent cantilever arms remain. Entire upper works are subsequently replaced. River level, fall tilt, joint plates, bolt patterns, support profiles and splashes are estimates. The 1160.8m context envelope is not a surveyed map or actual pier-spacing schedule outside the critical span.

## Sampoong records and remembrance review — 2026-11-17

Records first; the 3D scene and geometry are unchanged. Sources were re-read in a browser on 2026-11-17. Curated records live in `research/records/sampoong.json`; the generator attaches them.

- **Term correction.** The [Presidential Archives record](https://www.pa.go.kr/research/contents/policy/index15.jsp?poMode=view&policySeq=3045) (1A00614174634055, 1995-07-19) is titled 특별재해지역 선포 담화문, under the new Disaster Management Act. The demo had used the later term 특별재난지역; all five languages now use the 1995 term (special disaster area / 特別災害地域 / 特别灾害地区 / zona especial de desastre).
- **Disaster Management Act.** [Korean Law Information Center](https://www.law.go.kr/%EB%B2%95%EB%A0%B9/%EC%9E%AC%EB%82%9C%EA%B4%80%EB%A6%AC%EB%B2%95/(04950,19950718)): Act No. 4950, enacted and in force 1995-07-18. Articles 3 (state and local duties), 20–23 (emergency rescue headquarters; on-site command assigns tasks to rescue agencies and volunteers) and 30–32 (special disaster areas). The [National Archives essay](https://www.archives.go.kr/next/newsearch/listSubjectDescription.do?id=001042&sitePage=) also names 18 July 1995 and local disaster offices. Only the 1995 text is cited; later law is not.
- **Rescue together.** The presidential record describes firefighters, police, the military and all available people and equipment mobilised for rescue; the archive essay records volunteers pouring in, and also poor traffic control, missing drawings and press crowding. No head counts, rescuer names or rescue sequence are asserted.
- **Memory in Seoul.** [Seoul culture news 50141](https://news.seoul.go.kr/culture/archives/50141) (2014): the Seoul Foundation for Arts and Culture project with 15 citizen memory collectors recording survivors, witnesses and rescuers, recordings to be kept at Seoul Library and online. A plan-stage announcement; later interview totals and the resulting book were not verified here, and no testimony is reproduced. Named individuals in that article are not added.
- **Memorial.** [Seoul news hub 2002100](https://mediahub.seoul.go.kr/archives/2002100), a citizen-reporter article of 2021-06-30 (not an official city position): memorial in Yangjae Citizens' Forest about 5 km from the site, consoling the 502 victims; families' flowers and messages at the 26th anniversary. Construction and unveiling dates were not verified. The scene does not draw the memorial.
- The old `warning-care` record now covers warnings and the duty to evacuate only; the volunteer clause moved to the new rescue record. Park Seung-hyeon stays the first (default) record.
- Fixed the Seoul Institute source title (`June 1995 exterior photograph, code 01O01101Db8000`, checked at data.si.re.kr/node/52399) and spacing typos in these notes.
